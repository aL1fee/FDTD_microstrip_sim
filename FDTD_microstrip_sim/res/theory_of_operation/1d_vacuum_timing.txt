1D vacuum FDTD timing and playback

* Courant number (S) - Fraction of a cell a vacuum wave travels per step.
* Timestep (dt) - Physical duration of one Yee update.
* Simulated time (t) - Accepted steps multiplied by the timestep.
* Vacuum crossing (s) - Target on-screen travel time across the grid.

Physical model
--------------
The 1D Yee solver advances normalized electric and magnetic field samples
in homogeneous vacuum. Source amplitudes and field values are normalized.

    dx = cellSize * 1e-3        Grid spacing in meters (cellSize is in mm)
    c0 = 299792458 m/s          Vacuum wave speed
    S  = c0 * dt / dx = 0.5    Dimensionless Courant number
    dt = S * dx / c0            Physical time per accepted Yee step, in s
    t  = n * dt                 Simulated time after n accepted steps
    t_ps = t * 1e12             Simulated time shown in picoseconds

S is the update coefficient in both normalized field equations. The
simulation clock starts at zero, advances only when a Yee update runs,
and returns to zero on Reset. Step Once pauses playback and advances one
update. Run resumes from the current step after a pause.

Source timing
-------------
Sources inject after the electric update and before the magnetic update.
The update targeting step n evaluates the waveform at t = n * dt.

    dt_ref = S * (0.02 * 1e-3 m) / c0
    t0     = 40 * dt_ref         Gaussian center, about 1.334 ps
    sigma  =  8 * dt_ref         Gaussian standard deviation, about 0.267 ps
    Gaussian(t) = exp(-0.5 * ((t - t0) / sigma)^2)
    f_hz = f_ghz * 1e9          Frequency from the individual CW source
    CW(t) = sin(2 * pi * f_hz * t)

The reference spacing fixes the Gaussian's physical duration. Grid
refinement samples the same envelope with a smaller timestep. CW timing
uses the source frequency and the calibrated simulation clock.

Screen playback
---------------
Vacuum crossing (s) is the target time on screen for a vacuum wave to
travel from the first to the last electric sample. Its default is 2 s
and the slider spans 0.5 to 10 s.

    N = trunc(domain length in mm / cellSize)
    L = (N - 1) * dx             Distance between endpoint samples
    T_vac = L / c0               Ideal vacuum transit time in physical s
    crossing_steps = T_vac / dt = (N - 1) / S
    playback_rate = crossing_steps / crossingSeconds
                                 Yee steps per wall-clock second

Each running frame adds elapsed wall time times playback_rate to a step
budget. The app advances the whole steps and carries the fractional
remainder into the next frame. It caps the elapsed time used by one
frame at 0.25 s to limit catch-up after a stall, and uploads the field
display once after a batch. The slider affects the step budget. At equal
accepted step counts, the field values depend on the simulation setup.

With a 4.5 mm domain and 0.02 mm cells, N = 225 and L = 4.48 mm.
The timestep is about 33.36 fs, and the ideal transit takes about
14.94 ps or 448 steps. A 2 s screen crossing gives 224 steps/s.
The screen crossing is a reference based on c0. Finite-grid dispersion
can shift the observed pulse peak.

Verification
------------
A standalone vacuum test measured the delay between Gaussian pulse peaks
at probes 1 mm apart, before reflected signals could reach the probes.
The ideal delay was 3.335641 ps. The measured delays were 3.354599 ps at
0.020 mm spacing and 3.340551 ps at 0.010 mm spacing.

A separate harness produced byte-identical electric and magnetic traces
at equal accepted steps with 0.5 s crossing at 60 FPS and 4 s crossing
at 144 FPS. The frame rates were simulated.
